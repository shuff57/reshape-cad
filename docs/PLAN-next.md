## 33. S4h: coaxial sphere and cone

A sphere or a cone against a plane square to its polar axis (a box face, a slab), a cylinder about the same axis, or another sphere or cone about it used to refuse ("combine: brep-rs cannot boolean these two solids"), the largest class in the sweep. Every cut there is a circle of constant latitude (or height), so the face splits into whole bands, each classified whole. Built in `ops_planar.rs` as a fourth kind of operand face (`AFace::Rev`) beside the plane and the cylinder wall; the older face-by-face path still runs first and is unchanged.

- **Design.** One axis for the pair (`Fam`: a point, a direction, and the direction every circle starts at, so the two bands that meet on a circle agree on its seam vertex). A cone or a sphere zone fixes it; whole spheres have no axis of their own, so they take the line through their centres, and when that is no help (one centre) they try the axes the other faces suggest (a cylinder's, then a plate's) before refusing. A whole sphere is re-framed onto that axis. Each face is a band between two heights `z` (a sphere's colatitude and a cone's slant are monotone in `z`), cut at the heights where the other operand's faces meet it: a plane square to the axis whose circle lies wholly inside the plane face; a coaxial cylinder (`rho(z) = r`); another sphere or cone (`meet_profiles`: line against line, sphere against sphere, sphere against line, a quadratic at most). Plane faces get the circle as a cut (`rev_circle_on_plane`) and every piece is built with its own seam at angle zero (`rev_piece`: meridian arc or ruling, rim circles shared by handle after the weld, no rim at a pole or an apex). Classification is `inside_solid` at nine points of a band (three heights, three angles) which must agree; the subtract flip needed a sphere arm in `flip_face` (whole turn, `e2` negated, like the cone's). The existing guards all run (closed 2-manifold, translation invariance, `boolean_result_is_sound`, inclusion-exclusion with the partner operation to 1e-9) plus one new one: a result in more pieces than its operands could account for is refused (`too_many_pieces`: a cut that strands a tip, a sealed void), because this module builds every result as one shell. That keeps `what_cannot_be_exact_stays_a_refusal` (the cone bore that leaves a floating tip) a refusal.
- **Refuses, in the old sentence.** A plane that reaches the surface and is not square to the axis (a box side that cuts a ball, a ball against a box corner or edge: the face's box must stand clear of the ball); a cylinder off the axis, partial, trimmed, or with its circles starting elsewhere than the pair's; coincident surfaces (the same ball, the same cone); tangent surfaces; a plane through an apex or on a pole; a circle that meets or lies on an edge of the plane face it would cut; a pair that also holds a crossing bore or a tee; and any result in two pieces. Within 1e-7 mm a face is "the same place" as its neighbour (the module's one tolerance), so a slice thinner than that is not seen: a cone base 5e-8 into a cylinder top builds with 5.6e-6 mm^3 of that sliver missing (relative 5e-9); the near-tangent test allows exactly that and nothing else.
